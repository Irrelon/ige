# IGE 3D Renderer - Development Plan

## Summary

The goal is unchanged from the original plan: one renderer that draws 2D sprites and 3D models with the same entity API,
keeping IGE simple to use. The route has changed. Instead of building on Three.js, IGE now has its own WebGL renderer,
`IgeWebGlRenderer`, which is the primary 3D path. `IgeThreeJsRenderer` remains as an incomplete experiment and is not
being developed.

The current driver for renderer work is a **split-screen zombie ARPG** (up to four local players, large hordes). The
`src/examples/zombie-game` example is a playable prototype of that game and doubles as the renderer's integration test
and performance benchmark. `src/examples/hex-realms` (a large hex-map strategy prototype, in progress) is a second
test bed; its README lists the engine gaps found while building it (see "Engine gaps from Hex Realms" below).

Guides: [`guides/webgl-models.md`](guides/webgl-models.md) (3D models and characters),
[`guides/physics-3d.md`](guides/physics-3d.md) (Jolt physics: bodies, characters, ragdolls) and
[`guides/2d-on-webgl.md`](guides/2d-on-webgl.md) (moving a 2D project to the WebGL renderer).

## Why a custom WebGL renderer

- IGE's scene graph, viewports and transforms map directly onto the renderer; nothing has to be mirrored into a second
  scene graph.
- The frame pipeline can be shaped for IGE's needs, e.g. collecting the scene once and drawing it into several
  split-screen viewports.
- No large runtime dependency and a smaller bundle.
- Full control over shaders, batching and instancing.

The cost is that features Three.js provides for free (loaders, materials, post-processing) have to be built, so the
plan prioritises what the target games need.

## Where things stand (November 2026)

### Measured

On the development machine (Apple silicon, Chrome):

- Zombie demo, four viewports: 120 zombies at about 66 fps at a 1.5 pixel ratio. With 200 zombies the frame went
  from about 43 ms to about 15 ms after the instancing, culling and transform work.
- LOD cuts the demo's submitted triangles from about 4.1 to 1.9 million per frame (GPU time about a quarter lower);
  back-face culling cuts GPU time by about a fifth (7.7 to 6.0 ms with 60 zombies).
- Shadow caching: in Hex Realms a camera pan drops from 14-15 shadow draw calls per frame to 1; the zombie demo's shared
  shadow map from 29 to 5.
- Per-face point shadow culling: the lighting example from 130 to 51 shadow draw calls and 7.0 to 3.4 million
  triangles per frame; with its `?lod=1` option the shadow maps use lighter meshes and it drops to 1.6 million.
- Allocation-free transforms: with 300 zombies the update phase dropped from about 10 ms to 1.5 ms a frame and the
  demo from 29 to 60 fps; `IgeMatrix2d` built several temporary objects per moving entity per frame.
- Animation then costs 0.31 ms a frame for 300 animated characters and the renderer's CPU work about 1 ms, so lowering
  the update rate of distant characters isn't needed (decided 2026-11-09; revisit with denser rigs if profiling shows
  otherwise).
- Triangle BVH: a raycast against a 320,000-triangle terrain drops from 38.7 ms to 0.018 ms; projecting a decal onto
  it takes 0.55 ms; building the BVH takes about 0.46 s, once per mesh.

### Verified

- WebGL 1 (`?webgl=1` on the zombie and lighting examples): everything renders with no GL errors, including skinned
  characters, shadows, decals, particles and the overlay (two bugs found and fixed: shadows were blended away, and
  meshes over 65535 vertices didn't draw).
- Multiple viewports: the 4-view zombie demo, the 2-view 2D example (`webGl2d?split=1`), and racing-game's
  two-player split screen.
- Hex Realms runs on the current engine; back-face culling, shadow caching and render on demand were tried on it from
  the console (tiles style).
- Pointer events with a real mouse in the 2D split-screen example; mesh and skinned-character picking in the zombie
  demo's views. Sweeping sideways from a character's chest, triangle picking stopped at the body's edge (12 pixels out)
  while box picking still reported hits 24 pixels out; a raycast against the whole demo scene takes about 0.2 ms.
- Canvas 2D features on WebGL, compared side by side with `IgeCanvas2dRenderer` in `webGl2d` (`?canvas=1`): native
  and bitmap-font `IgeFontEntity` text, bound text, filters applied at load and later, an animated pre-filter, a
  composite-cached group and cached entities. The comparison found and fixed Canvas 2D bugs too: cached entities not at
  their parent's origin vanished, caches were blurry on high-DPI screens, and bitmap fonts were cut in half.
- Projected decals on the zombie demo's walls, pillars and furniture (WebGL 2 and 1), including wrapping a pillar's
  corner, and floor decals that stay off the pillar's sides.
- Jolt physics in the zombie demo, single-threaded and threaded (webpack build, cross-origin isolated page):
  characters, crates and ragdolls; after 15 seconds of play no ragdoll had fallen through the floor or flown off.
  Physics raycasts (points, normals, misses) and kinematic bodies checked from the console. Player characters walk
  at the set speed, stop at walls, climb a 20-unit step but not a 60-unit block, push crates and trigger sensors.

- Gamepads, with simulated controllers (`navigator.getGamepads()` replaced in the page): sticks move players at the
  set speed, the deadzone holds small stick values at zero, A, X and the right trigger attack once per press (not while
  held), and a disconnected pad stops its player.
- Physics layers (the living walk through corpses), compound and heightfield shapes, impact events, unpushable
  players, `discardGeometryArrays()` (still drawn and pickable) and `preTick` behaviours under WebGL, all in the
  browser.

### Not verified

- Gamepads with real controllers (the input path is verified with simulated ones).

## Next steps

### Needs your input

- **Game assets.** Zombie and player models with walk, run, attack, hit and death clips, to be generated with an
  online service. The engine side is ready: clips in separate files load through `model(..., { animations })`, dense
  meshes get `lod` levels, and weapons attach with `attachToBone()`. When the assets arrive, check that the rig and
  clips load and play, and whether an A-pose model needs clips authored for a T-pose rig (posture differences aren't
  corrected).
- **Gamepads.** Test with real controllers (simulated ones pass).

### Decided

- **Isometric 2D games move to a 3D layout** (ground on x/z, `isometric` camera preset) to run on WebGL. The engine
  won't map iso-mode x/y/z automatically; iso games that stay on x/y keep `IgeCanvas2dRenderer`.
- **Materials** have a public API: `entity.material({...})` (`_materialData` still works).
- **Physics runs on Jolt**, behind IGE's own `IgePhysics3d` interface (benchmark and spike below).

### Engine work remaining

None outstanding; everything above is done. Completed on 2026-11-09:

- Physics: heightfield and compound shapes, impact events, character contact rules (`pushable: false`) and named
  collision layers.
- Entity `preTick` behaviours run under the WebGL renderer, once per frame with a 2D context whose drawing calls do
  nothing (entities have no `postTick`; the engine's runs as before).
- A triangle BVH for raycasts and decal projection against large meshes.
- Animation update rates: profiled, not needed (see Measured).

## Physics engine options

Researched on 2026-11-08 (maintenance data from GitHub and npm on that date).

What the big engines use: Unity's built-in 3D physics is NVIDIA PhysX (Unity Physics or Havok for DOTS); Unreal Engine
5 replaced PhysX with its own Chaos; Godot ships Jolt and makes it the default for new 3D projects since 4.6. Jolt is
also used in Horizon Forbidden West and Death Stranding 2.

| Engine (npm) | Licence | Binding | Maintenance | Browser threads | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jolt (`jolt-physics`) | MIT | Emscripten WebIDL, by Jolt's author | Core v5.6.0 (Jul 2026), very active; npm 1.1.1 (Oct 2026) | Yes (needs cross-origin isolation) | Ragdolls with skeleton mapping, two character controllers, state save/restore. Manual `destroy()` and ref counting; tolerances in metres |
| Rapier (`@dimforge/rapier3d`) | Apache-2.0 | Rust, wasm-bindgen, hand-written TS | Core monthly; JS 0.21.0 (Sep 2026), separate repo archived Jul 2026 and moved into the core repo; JS releases uneven | No | Easiest to integrate (GC-friendly, `lengthUnit`, snapshots); no ragdoll helper |
| PhysX (`physx-js-webidl`) | BSD-3 | Emscripten WebIDL, one maintainer | 2.9.1 (Oct 2026) | No | Largest wasm (about 1.1 MB brotli); fastest single-threaded in one benchmark |
| Havok (`@babylonjs/havok`) | MIT binary, closed source | Emscripten, C-style API | 1.3.14 (Jul 2026) | No | Ruled out: not open source |
| ammo.js (Bullet), cannon-es | zlib, MIT | | No release since 2022 | No | Ruled out: unmaintained |

**Chosen: Jolt** (2026-11-08). The spike in the zombie demo (`?physics=1`, 120 zombies, about 25 ragdolls, 15
crates, around 450 bodies, four views) costs a 3.1 ms step single-threaded and 0.84 ms threaded; frames stay at the
no-physics 16-17 ms when threaded. See `guides/physics-3d.md`.

Newer options to revisit later: Box3D (from Box2D's author, v0.1, no official JS binding) and crashcat (pure TS,
modelled on Jolt, pre-1.0).

Benchmarked in Chrome on 2026-11-08 (`benchmarks/physics`, results in its README): milliseconds per step, mean.

| Scene | Jolt | Jolt, threaded | Rapier | Rapier SIMD | PhysX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500 zombie capsules | 2.63 | 0.94 | 1.68 | 0.94 | 0.88 |
| 1000 falling boxes | 5.08 | 1.75 | 2.26 | 1.56 | 2.04 |
| 100 ragdolls (11 bodies each) | 4.76 | 0.99 | 3.87 | 2.20 | 1.17 |
| 2000 raycasts | 2.54 | 1.78 | 1.67 | 1.69 | 2.51 |

PhysX is the fastest single-threaded engine and Rapier SIMD close behind; single-threaded Jolt is the slowest, but
threaded Jolt (which needs cross-origin isolation) is the fastest on ragdolls and level with the best elsewhere. All of
them handle the zombie game's load in a fraction of a frame, so features, API and maintenance should decide.

For the zombie game: simulate in metres and scale to IGE units when copying transforms; give the four players a
character controller and the horde cheap kinematic or rigid-body capsules; wrap every Emscripten object in IGE classes
that free it. The previous Jolt attempt most likely failed on units (Jolt's tolerances assume metres) and on manual
memory management.

## Engine gaps from Hex Realms

Status of the notes in `src/examples/hex-realms/README.md`:

| # | Gap | Status |
|---|-----|--------|
| 1 | Vertex colours ignored by the lit shaders | Done: geometry `colors` (RGB or RGBA) multiply the base colour |
| 2 | LOD only for model parts | Done: `lod()` works on `_geometryData` entities, generated (`ratio`) or authored (`geometry`) |
| 3 | No public way to free geometry or textures | Done: `renderer.releaseGeometry()` / `releaseTexture()`; `IgeTexture.destroy()` frees its GPU copy |
| 4 | Three copies of every geometry | Done: the engine keeps no interleaved copy, and `renderer.discardGeometryArrays(geometry)` frees the caller's arrays once uploaded (the bounding box is kept for culling and picking; LODs must be made first) |
| 5 | Shadow map redrawn every frame | Done: `renderer.shadowCaching(true)` |
| 6 | Back-face culling never enabled | Done, opt-in: `renderer.backFaceCulling(true)`; `doubleSided` materials are exempt |
| 7 | Picking only against a flat plane | Done: `renderer.screenToHeightfield(viewport, x, y, heightAt, { minHeight, maxHeight, step })` |
| 8 | Every geometry creation logged | Done: no longer logged |
| 9 | No automatic render-on-demand | Done: `ige.engine.renderOnDemand(true)`, which notices `material()`, `texture()`, `geometry()` and other setters. Hex Realms assigns `_geometryData` directly for chunk swaps; `entity.geometry(mesh)` makes those visible to it |
| 10 | Network transport | Open question (see below) |

## Open questions

- Networking: Hex Realms runs its own small protocol over the `websocket` package (rooms, seats, sequenced command
  broadcast) rather than `IgeNetIoServer`. Should the engine's networking grow those pieces?
- Unified asset loading (textures and models through one loader) is still only an idea.
- Should `IgeCanvas2dRenderer` remain the default for 2D projects, or should the WebGL renderer replace it?
